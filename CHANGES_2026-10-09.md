# 9 Oct 2026: the head editor (web/head.html, p5)

The isopod model's shaped head drawn as the sheet draws it, plan over elevation, with handles. p5.js 2.3.3 (LGPL 2.1)
is self-hosted at `web/static/p5.min.js` and used in instance mode; it owns one canvas and nothing else. Nothing in
joints/, instrument.py, mesh.py, anatomy/ or isopod_model/ changes.

## What it does
* `GET /head[?style=<name>]`: the page. Left: head type, the pinned handle's sliders (or every outline + face key),
  the list of edits (each undoable), copy JSON, build. Right: the drawing. Half-width = 1; front to the left; dotted
  lines are the first thorax segment's half-width (so `width` is visible); section on the axis solid, highest profile grey.
* Every drag or slider POSTs `/api/head_diagram` `{style, outline: {edits}, face: {edits}}`; the server runs
  `shaped_head.diagram` on the SAME Shape / head_crescent functions the solid is built from (~0.2 s) and answers the
  resolved outline + face, the polylines, the handle positions and `shaped_head.HANDLES` (which keys each handle drags).
  One request in flight at a time; the newest state always goes last. Unknown keys -> 400.
* The base face of a style is what the build would use: the default preset with the style's schema values through
  `body.face_of`, then the style's own face keys (so harpes draws with its tubercle eyes, not the crescent's 0.16).
* Handles: front (length, front_exp), process, widest (widest, width), angle (side, rear_taper), spine (genal length
  and spread, absolute: the tip follows the mouse), root (genal_at), notch, glabella (glab_len, glab_inflate), eye
  (eye_pos, eye_lat), brim (brim_w), crown (height, glab_rise; on the elevation). The other keys of each handle are
  sliders. Ranges are the page's (`R` in head.html): shaped_head.OUTLINE and head_crescent.FACE carry no ranges.
* Build: `/api/build` now takes `head` as a dict `{style, outline, face}` as well as a style name. `_head_custom`
  resolves it (style's outline + face, edits on top, validated), `_iso_key` keys it `<hash>-h-custom-<id>-<SHAPED_SIG>`
  (id = sha1 of the resolved numbers), `_build_isopod` builds `body.build(P, "shaped", face=, outline=)`. The page shows
  the pieces, bodies/watertight, and the STL links. `/api/sheet` passes the dict through unchanged.
* `GET /static/<file>` serves `web/static/` (send_from_directory: no traversal).

## Checks
* `tests/test_head_editor.py` (4): pages + static (+ traversal refused), a style's diagram face == `body.face_of` of
  the style's P, edits redraw (spine handle appears with the spine; shorter glabella ends further back) and bad keys
  -> 400, the custom key is its own and deterministic. Fast web suite: 22 passed.
* Headless browser (Chromium): page loads, dragging the harpes spine handle 90 px rearward writes
  `genal 1.30 -> 1.58`, the sliders pin, the all-keys list and a slider edit on phacops (`glab_len 0.5`) redraw.
* A build through the editor path (phacops, `genal 0.6, genal_spread 20, glab_len 0.55`): 11 watertight single
  bodies in 178 s, key `...-h-custom-8323e0cec4-...`; the solid shows the splayed spines and the shorter glabella.

## Limits
* "crescent" in the editor is the shaped twin of the crescent (shaped_head.OUTLINE defaults drawn whole), not the
  approved head on the isopod head piece; building it from the editor builds the shaped one.
* `brim_pits` exists in both OUTLINE and FACE; the page edits the outline one.
* `widest` and `angle` handles coincide on a head without a genal spine (both at the genal angle); the first wins.
* Drag gains are hand-set per handle; the live redraw is what keeps them honest. The thorax reference and the
  elevation's z scale are the diagram's (0.85 x usual head height), not the built animal's.
* Not done: the main page does not open an editor head (the share link / `?head=` carries style names only); the
  classic sketch in index.html is unchanged; no handles for sculpt keys without a plan position (bullae, caeca, node).

# 10 Oct 2026: the body editor (web/body.html) and the rows model (isopod_model/rows.py)

The body as ONE LIST OF ROWS, head to tail tip: the thorax rows and the pygidial ribs are the same thing, and the
pygidium is the trailing rows that have fused. Decided with Claire (10 Oct): a new tergite slab surface (not the pin
shingle), fusion automatic by print pitch, the pygidium = trailing fused rows. Print model only: anatomy/, joints/pin,
instrument.py, mesh.py untouched; the pin pipeline and the old isopod build (no `rows`) are unchanged.

## isopod_model/rows.py
* A row: len, width, axis, relief, margin, sweep, curve, tip, tip_w, arch, fulcrum, slope, rear_round, kind (ROW_KEYS,
  with units and docs). `rows_from_P(P, spec)` derives the preset's rows from the same schema keys thorax.plan and
  tail.plan read (width field re-sampled, pleural spine field, ring arch ...), so no edits = the preset. Body keys
  (BODY_KEYS): thorax_rows, pyg_rows, thorax_len, pyg_len, term_spine, min_joint_mm, print_scale.
* The pygidium is drawn with at least ceil(Lp / 0.55 pitch) rows so that at the preset's own print scale its rows are
  below the joint minimum and it comes out as ONE fused piece (the schema's pygRings counts the drawn ribs only).
* `groups(rows, scale, min_mm)`: the fusion rule. A row shorter than MIN_JOINT_MM (17 mm print: the isopod's own
  17.5 mm pieces are the smallest that carry a socket half and a ball half) is small; consecutive small rows fuse into
  one piece; a piece still shorter than the minimum fuses into the piece behind it. Defaults: every thoracic row its
  own piece, the pygidium one piece (textured 10 pieces, harpetida 10, redlichiida 15, agnostida 3).
* `tergite(row, next)`: the slab. A transverse vault (inner run nearly flat to the fulcrum, then the pleural drop), the
  ring arch on the axis, rising 6 % of relief over the row so the plate over the next row wedges up as the isopod's
  does; the rear edge carries OVERLAP = 0.45 of the NEXT row's pitch over it; a pleural spine as a tapering chord past
  the pleural edge; the last row's rear margin a half-ellipse (rear_round). No front drop, no hump along the row.
* `group_plan`: a fused group as one plan, the rows' slabs end to end, a furrow groove at each internal boundary, and
  the outline tapering with its rows (at |x| the first and the last row that reach |x|).
* `plans(P, spec)` gives body.py its [(name, mod, S, offset)] with variable pitch, the groups, the print scale
  (default 18 mm per median thoracic row, as before) and the terminal spine; `diagram(P, spec)` the editor's drawing.

## isopod_model/body.py
`build(P, head, face, outline, rows=spec)`: plans from rows.plans (row 0 where seg0 stood), joint gaps constant in
print mm, `iso_kit(P, s=)` at the spec's scale, and `build_part(..., shell=True)`: a WALL-thick slab everywhere except
the joint strip ahead of the rear joint plane (solid to the bed for the column). The terminal spine is unioned on the
last piece along the axis. `ROWS_INFO` carries the groups / scale of the last rows build.
Checks: the default through the rows path: 11 pieces, all watertight single bodies, NO rest contacts, none at 20 deg,

pygidium, a 12 mm terminal spine; `dalmanitid_spec.json`): 13 watertight pieces, no rest contacts, 242 x 586 x 57 mm.

## Known limits
* Ramp interpolates EVERY edited key of the pinned row, `len` included; pin a row with only the keys you mean.
* Changing thorax_rows keeps thorax_len, so more rows = shorter rows = below the joint minimum until thorax_len or
  print_scale is raised; the orange tells you.
* No per-row ornaments yet (axial spines, prongs) and no ventral/doublure; the head is the head editor's (its own
  genal spines), drawn here at the first row's width x the head/thorax ratio.
* Drag gains are hand-set; the drawing is the truth. Saved bodies, like saved heads, are server-wide and unauthenticated.

## stress battery and the guards it bought (2026-10-11)
Nineteen extreme bodies and heads through the rows model (isopod build, one subprocess each, 15 min timeout):
tiny rows (1.5 mm), one row, 60 rows, giant (600 x 150 mm rows), needle (2 mm wide), spiky (300 mm spines at 80 deg),
flat (0.5 mm relief), forward sweep -80 deg / curve 85, axis 0.9 / fulcrum 0.02 / slope 3, no pygidium + 500 mm
terminal spine, print scale 20 and 0.1, everything fused, a 3 x 3 x genal-3 head, a 0.2 x 0.3 head, pyg rows before
thorax rows, 100 mm spines at 60 deg, the largest allowed head, 300 mm wide rows.
What failed, and the fix:
- giant and spiky: out of memory. The front cutter sampled 0.1 mm over the whole part (600k points), and the head's
  ray grids 0.25 mm over a 600 mm head (5.8 M rays). Both grids now coarsen past a point budget (CUTTER_MAX_POINTS
  150k, HEAD_MAX_POINTS 400k) -- the default animal is under both, so it is unchanged. giant builds in 280 s, 3.4 GB.
- needle and print_scale 0.1: IndexError in isopod_head when a ray grid hits nothing. Guarded. But both are animals
  the joint cannot fit: the kit is 15 mm wide and 13.5 mm tall at print, absolute. rows.problems() now says so, and
  the print's size (rows + terminal spine) must stay under 700 mm. The editor still draws such a body and shows the
  reason (red, build disabled); /api/build answers 400 before building. Also caught: flat (the kit stood through the
  0.5 mm plates at every joint), tiny rows (a metre print), scale 20 (2.8 m), the terminal spine case (1.3 m).
- the 0.2-long head: ValueError in interp. shaped_head.outline_of now has OUTLINE_RANGE (finite, bounded, the
  silhouette's working ranges); the 3 x 3 x genal-3 head is out of range, and the largest allowed one is refused by
  the print-length estimate (head + spines + body > 700 mm) before a build.
- forward sweep and long swept spines build, but the clearances sever what overlaps the part ahead (730 mm3 on
  seg0, 270 mm3 on rear spines). That is now a named note in the manifest and print_valid is false (> 20 mm3 cut
  from a part), instead of "debris".
- mixed kinds (pyg before thorax): the 400 it was meant to be.
What held: one row (32 s), 60 rows (42 pieces, 168 s), everything fused (3 pieces, 36 s), the odd shapes (flat,
forward sweep, full axis), 300 mm rows (260 s), 100 mm spines (236 s): all watertight, single bodies, no contacts.
The default build is unchanged by all of this (volumes and bounds equal to the reference).

## build: head cache, manifolds through the stages, pooled cutters, 14-step bisection (2026-10-11, late)
Default preset: 94 s (start of the day) -> 46 s cold, ~43 s with the head cached. Parts within 0.0013 mm of the
reference surface (the bisection change), volumes within 0.08 mm3, bounds equal.
- the head is cached on disk (web/heads_cache/, 40 newest, TRILO_HEAD_CACHE to move it), keyed on what it reads:
  the preset, the face and outline, the first segment's reach and the tallest thorax piece (crescent) or the rows
  (shaped: its spines keep clear of the body), the first pivot, the kit scale, the gaps, and the head builders'
  source. The editor loop (same head, new rows) skips the 9 s head build.
- parts stay Manifold objects from the cutters to the clearances (no trimesh round trips between stages); flakes
  are dropped with decompose(); the stages are self-contained job functions on vertex/face arrays.
- the front cutters' lip shapes (numpy) are computed by forked workers (TRILO_WORKERS, default the core count up
  to 4; 1 = inline). The Manifold stages run inline: a forked child inherits TBB's pool state without its threads
  and a large boolean hangs (the giant case did), a spawned child re-imports the main module, and Manifold holds
  the GIL under threads. With more cores the cutters scale; the booleans do not, by design of this box.
- the neighbour clearances use the part ahead as it stands before its own clearance (identical unless a part's
  front overlaps the part behind -- the flagged forward-sweep case), so the stage no longer depends on order.
- BISECT_STEPS 24 -> 14 (Claire's verdict): the lip settles to 0.002 mm instead of 2e-6.
- giant stress case: 280 -> 229 s, peak 4.9 GB (the pooled cutters double that stage's memory).
